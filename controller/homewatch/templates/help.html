About homeWatch

A self-hosted monitor for your home: internet line, servers, network, backups and UPS, with notifications when something needs attention. It only reads from your devices and never changes them.

Install

You need a Linux host with Docker Engine and the Compose plugin (2 CPU, 2 GB RAM and 10 GB disk are plenty; add 2 GB of disk for Grafana). No Docker yet? Install it from Docker's own repository (https://docs.docker.com/engine/install/), then add your user to the docker group (sudo usermod -aG docker $USER) and log in again.

mkdir homewatch && cd homewatch
for f in docker-compose.yml Caddyfile env.example install.sh update.sh; do
  curl -fsSLO "https://github.com/nikosiaf/homewatch/releases/latest/download/$f"
done
chmod +x install.sh update.sh
less install.sh          # please read it first; it's short (q to quit)
./install.sh

The script writes .env with a new master key, creates a self-signed certificate, offers the updater (needs sudo once) and asks whether to add Grafana, then starts the containers. It prints the certificate's SHA-256 fingerprint: compare it when your browser warns about the certificate at https://<host>.

Open homeWatch and create the admin account right away: until it exists, whoever opens the page first creates it.

The setup wizard then asks for an admin account, your home's name and time zone, the internet checks and contracted speed, your equipment, a notification channel and an optional heartbeat. Under "Your equipment" you tick what you have; the wizard then opens each module's page in turn so you can connect it, or skip it. Anything else you add later, one module at a time, under Modules.

How alerts work

  • Confirmation: a change must hold for 2 runs (about 4 minutes) before it's sent, so short blips stay quiet.
  • Incidents: problems that start together (a new one within 30 minutes of the first, while it is still open) are one incident. Each problem is still sent as it comes, but "back to OK" waits and arrives as one message when the whole incident is over, with every check's times. If part of it stays open, what is already back to OK is sent after 5 minutes anyway. History lists the incidents with their timeline.
  • Likely cause: modules also note what their devices did, with the device's own time where it tells it: reboots (from uptime), DSL line resyncs and modem reboots, the UPS going on battery (its status is asked every 10 seconds, so a power cut of half a minute is caught), MikroTik ports losing link, OPNsense gateways going down. When an incident ends, homeWatch reads every device once more (about 30 seconds later) and names the likely cause from these events, for example a power cut in the area (UPS on battery, the modem stayed on, the DSL line re-synced), a modem restart, a line or ISP problem, or a restart of your own equipment. The cause and the events behind it are in the "back to OK" message and on the History page.
  • Times: an internet outage starts and ends when the ping data shows it, not when the next check run saw it. When a check starts to recover, every device is read again and the recovery is confirmed in a quick extra run about 30 seconds later; problems keep the normal pace, so short blips still stay quiet.
  • Grafana: every dashboard shows incidents (start and end, with the likely cause) and what the devices did as markers. A marker sits within 2 minutes of its time; its text has the exact time.
  • To do: updates and firmware (Home Assistant, PBS, DSM, UniFi, RouterOS and RouterBOOT, OPNsense) are things to do, not problems. They are listed under To do on the Status page and in the weekly report, and become a WARNING only when they have waited too long: 14 days, 7 for OPNsense and RouterOS (change it under the module's Checks and limits; empty = never).
  • Events, not warnings: one DSL resync or one switch, access point reboot is an event and a Grafana marker. A WARNING comes only when it repeats: 3 resyncs or 2 reboots in 24 hours by default.
  • Flapping entities: a Home Assistant entity that went unavailable 3 times in 7 days and came back by itself within 2 hours each time (phone and cloud sensors often do) is named under Unavailable entities but not counted. Untick "Leave out entities that keep flapping" in the module's settings to count every one.
  • Useful or noise: on History, mark a problem as worth telling you or as noise. The weekly report lists the checks with the most issues and the ones marked as noise, so you know which limits to change.
  • Reminders: open problems are repeated every 12 hours. Change this, the confirmation and how often checks run under Timing on the Notifications page.
  • Offline queue: messages that can't be sent while the line is down go out when it's back, with the outage times.
  • Snooze: holds one check's notifications for a while, from Maintenance or next to the problem on Status. The history is still recorded.
  • Maintenance windows: hold notifications for chosen checks, once or every week.
  • Status page: one tile per module, coloured by its worst check. Click one to see its checks. One stays open at a time, until you close it, open another or reload the page.
  • History: a timeline of every problem and its return to OK, by module, level and period.

Notifications

  • Telegram: a bot token and chat ID.
  • ntfy: one topic URL.
  • Email: any SMTP server. Port 465 uses TLS, other ports STARTTLS; a password is never sent unencrypted.
  • Webhook: every message is POSTed as JSON with text, content, title, body, level and level_name. It works as-is with Slack, Mattermost, Rocket.Chat and Discord.
  • healthchecks.io heartbeat: homeWatch pings it after every run, and healthchecks.io alerts you when the pings stop, for example when the host, power or internet is down.
  • Quiet hours per channel: warnings wait until they end; errors are always sent.
  • Weekly report: optional; pick the day, the time and the channels.

Settings and accounts

  • Accounts: an admin can change everything; a viewer can only see status, history and this page.
  • Configuration versions: every saved change is kept (last 200), showing who changed what. Each can be viewed and restored, and a restore can itself be undone.
  • Export and import: System downloads all settings as one YAML file, and one is written every night (last 14 kept). Importing replaces the settings; the previous ones stay under Configuration versions. Import and restore first show what would change and which credentials would be deleted, and ask for your password.
  • Passwords, tokens and users are never part of a version or an export. Enter them again after importing elsewhere.

Update and back up

The Home box on the System page says when a newer release exists. If install.sh added the updater, press Install there: homeWatch restarts with the new release within a minute or two. The updater runs on the host; homeWatch only asks it, and it always installs the latest release from GitHub.

Without the updater, set HW_VERSION in .env to the new release, then run:

docker compose pull && docker compose up -d

Database changes apply on start.

To back up, keep the Docker volumes homewatch_hw-data (settings, users, history, encrypted secrets) and homewatch_vm-data (metrics), and a copy of .env. Without HW_MASTER_KEY from that file, saved passwords and tokens can't be decrypted and you would enter them again. With Grafana, also keep homewatch_grafana-data (your own dashboards; the built-in ones are recreated).

Grafana dashboards

Grafana is optional. It shows everything homeWatch stores (every device, port, guest, disk and the Home Assistant entities you show in Grafana) as dashboards at https://<homeWatch>/grafana/. It has no login of its own: you are signed in with your homeWatch account. Admins are Grafana admins, viewers can only look.

  • Turn it on or off on the System page (Grafana and history). The first start downloads Grafana (about 2 GB) and it uses about 150 MB of memory. Turning it off keeps its dashboards. Without the updater: set COMPOSE_PROFILES=grafana in .env (empty to turn it off) and run docker compose up -d.
  • Built-in dashboards (folder homeWatch): an Overview and one per enabled module. They are rebuilt with every update, so they can't be saved. Switching a module off removes its dashboard.
  • Your own dashboards: open a built-in one, choose Save as and save it in My dashboards. Updates never touch that folder. homeWatch saves it every night next to the configuration exports (System → Grafana and history, last 14); to restore one, Dashboards → New → Import and paste its entry.
  • Home Assistant: only entities you tick under Entities on the module's page are stored (Watch or Show in Grafana), every time the module reads (2 minutes by default); the dashboards show the Show in Grafana ones and the host's CPU, memory and disk. While Grafana is off the column is hidden and the ticked entities are still stored, so their history has no gap.
  • Resolution follows each module's Read every setting: a module read every 10 minutes has one point per 10 minutes.
  • Data: the data source homewatch is VictoriaMetrics (PromQL / MetricsQL). Metric names start with hw_; Explore (left menu) lists them.
  • Older installs: Grafana needs this release's docker-compose.yml, Caddyfile and update.sh next to install.sh (the updater only changes the version). Download them from the release, then run docker compose up -d and, with the updater, sudo ./update.sh once (it tells homeWatch that it can now turn Grafana on and off).

History length: measurements are kept for a year by default. You can choose a shorter time under System → History: data older than that is deleted (VictoriaMetrics drops whole months, so space can take up to a month to come back). Issues, speed tests and settings are not affected.

Backup and recovery

  • HW_MASTER_KEY in .env decrypts every stored password and token. Keep a copy somewhere other than this host, such as a password manager.
  • Without that key the data still opens, but no stored credential can be read: modules show "WrongMasterKey" until you enter each credential again.
  • The README has the commands to back up and restore.

How to read the numbers

  • Internet availability counts only the time homeWatch was really watching the line. Time before the install, or with homeWatch or its probes down, is unknown and isn't counted as up. Monitored shows how much of the period that covers.
  • UNKNOWN on the Status page means a module has nothing to judge yet, or its reading can't be trusted.
  • A change must be seen in a new reading to count as confirmed. A module that reads rarely is read again sooner when a reading shows a change.

Security

  • Don't forward homeWatch to the internet. Use it on your LAN or over a VPN such as Tailscale.
  • Give each module a dedicated read-only account, as its setup steps describe.
  • Keep "Check the HTTPS certificate" on when the device has a trusted certificate. For a self-signed one, untick it: homeWatch trusts the certificate it sees at the first connection and logs in only to that one. Check the fingerprint on the module's page against the device. System lists how every device connection is protected.
  • Secrets, including ntfy topic and healthchecks.io ping addresses, are encrypted at rest, never shown again after saving and left out of exports. Passwords are hashed with Argon2. Logins lock for 5 minutes after 5 failures from one address, or 20 on one account.
  • Adding or removing a user and deleting data ask for your password again. Sessions are stored only as hashes, so a copy of the database can't be used to log in.
  • Behind a reverse proxy, name it in HW_TRUSTED_PROXIES in .env. Otherwise every visitor counts as the proxy's address for login lockouts.
  • When an import or restore changes where a credential goes, or turns off HTTPS or certificate checks, homeWatch deletes that credential and switches the module or channel off until you enter it again.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
"HTTPS certificate isn't trusted"Install a trusted certificate on the device, or untick "Check the HTTPS certificate" if it is self-signed.
ISP modem: "certificate is not the trusted one"homeWatch stopped logging in to the modem. If you replaced the modem or updated its firmware, press "Trust the new certificate" on the module page. If not, check your network first: another device may be posing as the modem.
A module answers with a 503 or isn't recognizedCheck the address is the device's web GUI and includes its port, such as https://192.168.1.1:8443. Port 443 may belong to a reverse proxy.
MikroTik: "can't reach the REST API"Check that www-ssl (or www) is enabled and that this host is in its available-from list.
OPNsense: "lacks the privilege"Add the named privilege to the homewatch user under System → Access → Users.
"Probe data fresh" is in errorCheck that the Telegraf container is running. If the host's clock jumps at boot, Telegraf stalls: on a Proxmox VM, set "Use local time for RTC" to No.
The internet checks show nothingCheck that the Internet line module is enabled and at least one probe is ticked.
Home Assistant doesn't report an entityOnly entities you tick under Watch (Modules → Home Assistant → Entities) are checked; nothing is ticked at first. Automations, scripts, scenes, buttons, cameras and similar domains aren't listed.
A device's check is often down on purposeUntick it in the module's lists, or switch the check off under Checks and limits on the module's page.
